## Research Frameworks

🔹 Finite Lifetime Convexity Optimization (FLCO)

A quantitative capital allocation framework designed for individuals operating under finite and uncertain human lifetime constraints.

Traditional portfolio optimization models implicitly assume long or indefinite horizons and prioritize volatility-adjusted performance metrics. FLCO reformulates the objective function by explicitly incorporating lifetime uncertainty as a stochastic constraint in the decision process.

Instead of maximizing return per unit of variance, FLCO defines the optimization objective as:

**Maximize:**  
`P( τ_π ≤ T )`

Where:  
- `τ_π` = stopping time required for strategy `π` to reach a predefined wealth target  
- `T` = remaining lifetime modeled as a random variable

Under this framework, strategy evaluation emphasizes:  
- Positive convexity and asymmetric payoff structures  
- Geometric growth rate (GHPR)  
- Probability of ruin and capital survivability  
- Inter-arrival time of extreme gains  
- Cross-regime robustness

FLCO integrates stochastic growth theory, convex payoff dynamics, and mortality-adjusted decision modeling into a unified framework for accelerated wealth transformation.

🔹 EFM Framework — Quantitative Research & Collaboration Opportunity

End Flow Momentum (EFM) is a proprietary quantitative framework developed through more than seven years of independent empirical research. Implemented in MQL5 for MetaTrader 5, the system combines market-regime analysis with an adaptive capital-exposure mechanism.

Unlike conventional approaches focused primarily on predicting price direction, EFM investigates changes in the statistical distribution of market movements. Its capital-allocation architecture incorporates a self-referential component that adjusts exposure in response to structural changes in the strategy's own account-balance series.

Empirical Validation

EFM has undergone a multi-method statistical evaluation using historical XAUUSD (M5) simulations, including:

Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio (PSR) and Minimum Track Record Length (MinTRL)

Structural break detection and multi-method statistical consensus

Walk-forward and out-of-sample evaluation

Block-bootstrap Monte Carlo simulations and tail-risk analysis

Dynamic robust risk measures and conditional position-sizing analysis

The research identified statistically significant differences in movement magnitude and dispersion under certain detected regimes, while directional predictability was not statistically established.

The evaluation also identified substantial tail-risk exposure associated with the framework's dynamic position-sizing mechanism, providing a defined area for further refinement.

These findings are based on historical simulations, not verified live investment performance. Model-selection bias and other validation limitations remain relevant.
